Planning & Procurement System

Rebuilding a stalled hospital system around the plan itself

A government hospital plans each year in two linked documents: the Annual Operations Plan (AOP), which sets each unit's objectives and activities, and the Project Procurement Management Plan (PPMP), which lists what has to be bought to carry them out. I redesigned the system meant to move both out of Excel after its development had stalled.

The Problem

Staff still planned in Excel because the system meant to replace it had stalled for six months inside one overloaded form.

The Solution at a Glance

Separate levels, separate saves

The plan is split into objectives, activities, resources and responsible persons, each on its own page.

Read the reasoning

Items from a library

Resources are picked from the hospital's item library, with expense class and price already attached.

Read the reasoning

Lock, then submit together

Finalizing the AOP locks it and generates the PPMP. Both go out as one request.

Read the reasoning

Background

One plan, spread across spreadsheets and paper

Each unit filled in spreadsheets more than a dozen columns wide, scrolling sideways and down to complete every row. The width was a nuisance. The bigger problem was that the two documents didn't know about each other.

No traceability

A PPMP item wasn't tied to its AOP objective. Finding out why it was requested meant searching by hand.

Duplicates

Items were retyped each time they were needed, and the same item ended up entered more than once.

Manual totals

Budgets were calculated and checked by hand.

Paper approvals

Each approving office read a printed copy, signed it, and passed it on to the next.
